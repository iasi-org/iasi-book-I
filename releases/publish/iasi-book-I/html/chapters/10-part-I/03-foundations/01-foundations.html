21  IASI Foundations

Candidate set for review

Author

Javier G. Grandez

Published

September 3, 2026

22 Purpose

This document reconstructs the current candidate Foundations of IASI.

The number is not predetermined. Ten candidates have survived the first pass, but each must still withstand reduction, fusion and challenge.

A Foundation is not a recommendation, workflow or rule. It is a premise adopted by IASI that constrains the principles, rules, methods and materializations derived from it.

Test: if removing the statement changes the nature of IASI, it is probably a Foundation.

23 01. Engineering Is the Purpose

The objective is good engineering. Intelligent Systems, automation, tools and methodologies are means, not ends.

23.1 Implication

IASI must not become an AI methodology, an automation methodology or a tool-centric methodology.

24 02. The Human Retains Responsibility

Augmentation can extend the engineer’s effective capabilities, but responsibility for engineering decisions and their consequences remains human.

24.1 Implication

Delegating execution does not delegate engineering responsibility.

25 03. Intelligent Systems Are Participants, Not Authorities

Intelligent Systems may participate throughout engineering work, but goals, priorities and acceptance remain under human authority.

25.1 Implication

Participation can be extensive without turning the Intelligent System into the accountable decision-maker.

26 04. Generation Is Not Validation

Producing or materializing a result does not demonstrate that the result is correct, suitable or accepted.

26.1 Implication

IASI distinguishes between:

implemented
validated
accepted

These states are related, but they are not equivalent.

27 05. Uncertainty Is Intrinsic

Engineering with generative Intelligent Systems cannot assume deterministic or reproducible construction processes. Variability and uncertainty belong in the engineering model.

27.1 Implication

Uncertainty must be designed for rather than treated as an exceptional defect.

28 06. Knowledge Must Persist

Knowledge required beyond the immediate interaction must be made explicit, persistent, versionable and traceable.

28.1 Implication

Conversation can support thinking, exploration and decision-making. Persistent artifacts preserve what the engineering process needs to retain.

Documentation is therefore one possible materialization of persistent engineering knowledge, not the Foundation itself.

29 07. Need Precedes Solution

Engineering begins by identifying the need or problem. Tools, technologies, methodologies and existing solutions are evaluated only afterwards.

29.1 Implication

A project may need work tracking without needing Jira.

It may need coordination without needing Scrum.

It may need versioned artifacts without requiring a particular repository platform.

The need determines what must be solved. Existing solutions do not determine how the problem must be expressed.

30 08. Everything Is Open to Question

No decision, practice, methodology, tool or IASI construct is protected from challenge merely because it already exists or has worked before.

30.1 Implication

IASI itself must remain evolvable.

Existing decisions may be retained, refined or replaced when better engineering evidence appears.

31 09. Engineering Must Remain Technology-Independent

The conceptual engineering model must not depend on a particular model, provider, tool, platform or technological generation.

31.1 Implication

Materializations may be technology-specific.

The underlying engineering concepts should survive the replacement of the technologies used to implement them.

32 10. Human Capacity Is a System Constraint

The sustainable speed of augmented engineering is constrained by the human capacity to understand, decide, review, validate and assimilate results.

32.1 Implication

Agent availability and execution speed do not imply unlimited human availability.

Automation should reduce mechanical work rather than convert every removed pause into another decision for the human.

33 Candidates deliberately not promoted

33.1 The Model Is Not the System

Currently considered a consequence of technology independence and of the semantic definition of an Intelligent System.

33.2 Failure Is Part of the Design

Currently considered derivable from Generation Is Not Validation and Uncertainty Is Intrinsic.

33.3 Control Is Designed In

Currently considered a strong Principle derived from human responsibility, human authority and uncertainty.

33.4 Documentation Is Part of the System

Currently considered better generalized by Knowledge Must Persist.

Documentation is one possible persistent materialization of knowledge.

33.5 Augmented Engineering amplifies capabilities, but can also amplify defects

This remains an important consequence of augmentation, but has not yet been demonstrated to be an irreducible premise.

34 Next review

The next pass should attempt to destroy this set rather than complete it.

Each candidate should be tested for:

  • dependency on another Foundation;
  • redundancy;
  • scope;
  • whether it is genuinely an irreducible engineering premise;
  • whether it is actually a derived Principle.

The final number of Foundations must emerge from the model.

It must not be selected for editorial symmetry.
